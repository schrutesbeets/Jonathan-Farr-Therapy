## 2. Existing Homepage Copy (`/`)

### Hero & Core Hook
- **Eyebrow:** Online therapy in Massachusetts for high-achievers spinning on the broken record of imposter syndrome
- **Headline:** You’re never enough. Over and over again.
- **Body:** Maybe it's how much you make, how much you do, or how hard you work. Maybe it's someone else telling you that. Maybe it's your own inner voice. In any case, you're suffering — either because you're doing everything you can, or you're struggling to do anything at all. And you feel like a failure.
- **Subhead:** You’re actually good at what you do, but you struggle to believe it.

### Populations Called Out
You’re under constant pressure to perform, and it’s getting very uncomfortable. You may be:
1. **An aviation professional:** a pilot, air traffic controller, or flight attendant juggling a busy schedule, the guilt of being away from family, and the mounting stress of the job (that you don’t think you can reveal to anyone)
2. **A mid-level or upper-level manager**, with the weight of a big product launch on your shoulders
3. **A performer:** a musician, actor, TV presenter, or journalist fighting performance anxiety
4. **A creative person** — an artist, designer, or advertising executive crippled by perfectionism
5. **An athlete**, professional or otherwise, haunted by the harsh words of an unforgiving coach
6. **A child of a narcissistic or abusive parent**, stuck in cycles of people-pleasing and fear of abandonment

### The Autocross / "Tire Chirp" Metaphor
- *“Pressure makes diamonds,” you tell yourself. But there’s a problem with that. Pressure doesn’t just make diamonds. It cracks them, too.*
- Everything, and everyone — even you — has limits. And you’re here because it feels like you’re approaching them, but you don’t know exactly where they are. Or you were told you didn’t have any.
- **What if you could live life *at* or *near* the limit, instead of constantly trying to push past it?**
- The tires on your car only have so much grip. Just before they lose that grip, they tell you — you’ll hear them start to chirp. Chances are, that’s where you are right now, and it’s a frightening place. You’re afraid you’re going to lose that grip completely and spin out… or crash. Maybe you already have.
- **Wait — so are you saying I just have to accept my limitations?**  
  Absolutely not. You need to use your awareness of your limitations to get you around the racetrack of life. In fact, it’s that very chirping of the tires that helps a race car driver know when they’re at the limit—and knowing that limit actually gets them around the track *faster*. The same awareness that keeps a driver from crashing, or an athlete from getting injured, is actually *what makes growth and improvement possible*. Developing that awareness is what I do.

### Boundaries & Belief Transformation
- Awareness of your limits enables you to set **boundaries**. Imagine if you established one boundary in your life right now:
  - *What would I be able to accomplish?*
  - *What would it be like if I wrote the story of my own life?*
  - *Who could I connect with more fully, more deeply?*
- **Setting boundaries means changing the status quo—not from the outside, but from within.**
- I will help you question and change the way you think: your beliefs, your conclusions, your hidden agendas you weren’t even aware of.
- I will help you turn your **suffering** into **struggling**, which ultimately leads to progress: **self-empathy, courage** (even when you’re afraid), and **healing**.
- **Closing Hook:** *Your inner voice SUCKS. It’s time to change it.*

## 3. Existing About Page Copy (`/about`)

### Headline: "I was an imposter, too."
- I have a bachelor's degree in film production, but that was the beginning of my own imposter syndrome. Directors had “vision,” I thought, of the grand movies they made. The only vision I had was being a production assistant standing at 28th and Broadway trying to stop traffic with nothing but a two-way radio… and no health insurance.
- I was a resident assistant in college, though, and I really loved that role — building community and, especially, talking with students one on one about their problems and lives. When a supervisor told me about getting a master's degree in higher education, I went for it.
- Four years later, I was the director of a residence hall, and a student approached me in the cafeteria. “Can we just... talk?” he asked. After making sure he was aware of the university's counseling center, he still chose me. It was the moment in my life when the **signal** of belief — in my own abilities — finally began to drown out the **noise** of imposter syndrome.
- A couple years later, I enrolled in another master's program — this time in counseling psychology — and here I am.
- The noise of my own imposter syndrome hasn't gone away; it probably never will. But in my own journey, I've chosen to focus on growing and amplifying that signal of belief.

### Client Fit Criteria
**I work best with clients who…**
- Have a quirky sense of humor (or like people that do)
- Enjoy metaphors and parallels to illustrate ideas and concepts
- Don’t mind the odd dad joke
- Are serious about change, but flexible about how to get there

**I’m probably not the therapist for you if…**
- You’re dealing with severe symptoms, including but not limited to: psychosis (hallucinations or delusions), mania, grandiosity, or you have a personality disorder
- You think the world owes you something
- You’re not willing to look seriously at your choices, beliefs, and patterns, and decide what you want to change
- You don’t like swearing
